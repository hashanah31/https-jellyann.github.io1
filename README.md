The Human Factor: Analyzing the Role of Social Engineering in Information Security Breaches at PHINMA University of Iloilo

I. Introduction
A. About the Organization
PHINMA University of Iloilo is a private university located in Iloilo City, Philippines. It is part of the PHINMA Education Network (PEN), which provide access and quality education to Filipino students. Founded in 1947 as Iloilo City Colleges, it became the University of Iloilo in 1968 and was later gained by PHINMA Education in 2009. The institution offers various programs including Basic Education, Business, IT, Engineering, Education, Criminology, Nursing, and Graduate Studies. PHINMA UI continues to keeps a vision of transforming students’ lives through education and developing professionals who can contribute to the community.
B. Why Choose this Organization
PHINMA University of Iloilo was chosen for this study because it is a leading academic institution that connect  technology into its daily operations. With the use of the Student Information System (SIS), online learning platforms, and digital communication tools, PHINMA UI encounter potential cybersecurity threats. One major concern is the weakness of students, faculty, and staff to social engineering attacks such as phishing, pretexting, and baiting. Analyzing the human factor in the university’s cybersecurity position will help identify areas where awareness, training, and behavior can be improved.

II. Purpose of the Project
The purpose of this project is to analyze the role of social engineering in information security breaches at PHINMA University of Iloilo. This study seeks to identify how human behavior contributes to cybersecurity incidents and propose solutions to strengthen awareness and reduce susceptibility. The research focuses on promoting a culture of cybersecurity among students, faculty, and staff to prevent manipulation through deceptive tactics.
III. Identified Risk
A. Risk Management Framework
Step 1: Categorize Information Systems:
PHINMA University of Iloilo uses digital systems such as the Student Information System (SIS), Learning Management System (LMS), and institutional email accounts. These systems store sensitive information that can be compromised through social engineering attacks. Common threats include phishing, impersonation, and baiting attempts targeting both faculty and students.
Step 2: Select Security Controls:
Physical Controls: Conduct regular seminars and awareness campaigns on recognizing and avoiding social engineering tactics. Display posters and infographics around campus promoting cybersecurity best practices.
Technical Controls: Implement advanced email filtering, multi-factor authentication (MFA), and endpoint protection to prevent unauthorized access. Regularly update software and enforce strong password policies.
Administrative Controls: Establish a Social Engineering and Phishing Policy. Implement access control, data privacy, and incident response policies to manage risks effectively.
Step 3: Implement Security Controls:
Train students, faculty, and administrative personnel to identify phishing attempts and suspicious activities. Deploy verification systems for identity checks when handling sensitive information.
Step 4: Assess Security Controls:
Conduct mock phishing simulations and vulnerability assessments to test awareness levels and the effectiveness of implemented security controls.
Step 5: Authorize Information Systems:
The IT Services Department (ITSD) should authorize and maintain compliance with security protocols, ensuring that all updates and controls are documented.
Step 6: Monitor Security Controls:
The ITSD should continuously monitor system logs, user access, and reported phishing attempts to evaluate new and evolving threats.
